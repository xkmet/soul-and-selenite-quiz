Getting Ready for Autumn

The light changes first — lower, softer, gold instead of white. Then the mornings cool, and something in us wants to slow down, gather in, and tidy up the year so far. This is a gentle guide to meeting that shift on purpose: a few crystals that suit the season, some simple practices, and a short reflection for the equinox.

What autumn asks of us

Autumn is a season of letting go — trees do it visibly, dropping what they no longer need so they can rest through winter. The autumn equinox, around 22–23 September each year (in 2026 it falls on 23 September), is the point when day and night are the same length, just before the dark half of the year takes over. Modern pagan tradition calls it Mabon and treats it as a harvest festival; the equinox itself has been marked far longer than that name has.

Six crystals for autumn

Smoky quartz, hematite, black tourmaline, citrine, amber and amethyst.

  • Smoky Quartz — for letting go. A soft, smoky-grey quartz traditionally used for releasing what you're carrying: old patterns, low-level stress, the residue of a hard few months. The obvious choice for a season that's all about shedding.
  • Hematite — for coming back down to earth. Genuinely heavy and cool in the hand — it's an iron ore. A grounding stone for pulling your attention back into your body on the days the shorter light leaves you scattered.
  • Black Tourmaline — for drawing your boundaries. As the nights close in and everyone retreats indoors, this is the classic protective stone — the one people keep by the door or on a desk as a quiet "not today" to other people's moods.
  • Citrine — for gratitude and the second harvest. Bright and warm, long associated with abundance. Mabon is a harvest festival, and citrine suits the part of autumn that's about noticing what you've gathered, not only what you've lost.
  • Amber — for gathered wisdom. Not strictly a crystal — it's fossilised tree resin, often tens of millions of years old, which is a fittingly autumnal thing to hold. Warm, golden, and traditionally a stone of patience and slow, considered decisions.
  • Amethyst — for turning inward. Deep violet and calming, a stone for a quieter mind. Good company for the reflective, slightly hibernatory mood that shows up once the clocks go back.

A few suggested poses

Autumn is a good time to move a little more slowly and closer to the ground. These are all beginner-safe and need nothing but a bit of floor space.

  • Mountain Pose — stand tall with your feet hip-width and your weight even, arms relaxed at your sides. The most literal "rooting down" pose there is; a steady way to begin, feeling the floor properly under you.
  • Tree Pose — balance on one foot with the other resting against your calf or inner thigh (not the knee), a hand on a wall if you want it. A pose about staying rooted while everything around you is changing.
  • Yogi Squat — feet a little wider than your hips, sink into a low squat with your heels down (a folded blanket under your heels if they lift), palms together at your chest. Hips heavy toward the floor — deeply grounding.
  • Seated forward fold — sit with your legs out in front and fold gently forward from the hips, bending your knees as much as you like. A quiet, inward fold, done from already sitting on the ground.

There's a companion gentle yoga guide with short video tutorials for a few more poses. Keep all of it soft — no strong twists, deep backbends, or inversions. If something doesn't feel right, stop.

A simple equinox reflection

The equinox is a natural checkpoint — you're two-thirds of the way through the year, with a clear pause before winter. You don't need anything for this beyond ten quiet minutes and something to write with.

Look back. What has this year actually given you so far? Name a few things — a skill, a friendship, a lesson, a habit that stuck. This is the harvest part, and most people walk straight past it.

Let something go. What are you ready to stop carrying into the darker months? Write it down plainly. You don't need to burn it or bury it — naming it is the practical bit. If you like a small ritual, fold the paper away somewhere out of sight until spring.

Set one gentle intention. Not a goal — an intention, and ideally one that fits the season. "Rest without guilt." "Cook properly again." "Be in bed by eleven." Autumn intentions work best when they're about slowing down, not speeding up.

This is a guide for reflection and seasonal ritual, not therapy or medical advice — crystals aren't a substitute for care you actually need. Take what's useful and leave the rest. (We use "autumn" throughout; "fall" is the same season. If you're in the southern hemisphere, save this one for March.)
